I am a computational researcher and PhD candidate at NYU Tandon, advised by <a href="https://engineering.nyu.edu/faculty/katepalli-sreenivasan" rel="external nofollow noopener" target="_blank">Prof. K. R. Sreenivasan</a>. I leverage high-performance parallel computing and Direct Numerical Simulations (DNS) to study the fundamental physics of turbulent flows. My doctoral research focuses on decaying turbulence, investigating the temporal evolution of kinetic energy, dissipation, and length scales, along with their sensitivity to initial conditions. To analyze these large-scale simulation datasets, I integrate statistical machine learning techniques and develop data-driven surrogate models for DNS—evaluating where ML/AI architectures sit on the Pareto trade-off between predictive accuracy and computational cost.

Prior to NYU, I completed an Masters of Technology (Research) in Computational Science at the Indian Institute of Science (IISc), Bangalore, where I worked on high-fidelity reactive flow simulations.

When not running simulations, I can be found outdoors trekking, camping, or on the field playing football, badminton, and cricket.
